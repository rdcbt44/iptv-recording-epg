## What Does “IPTV With Recording” Actually Mean?

There are three different things that often get thrown into the same bucket.

**EPG** is the electronic programme guide. It tells you what is playing now and what comes next.

**Catch-up TV** lets you replay programmes that have already aired, where catch-up is available.

**Recording** means your IPTV player actually saves the incoming stream so you can watch it later.

Those are not interchangeable.

TiviLive currently advertises a full EPG across its plans, and its August 2026 terms also describe the service as including catch-up TV. Its own IPTV-player guide specifically describes scheduled recording as a feature of compatible players such as TiviMate, while also highlighting players with recording functions such as 9Xtream.

So, for an **IPTV with recording** setup, the practical combination is:

**TiviLive subscription + recording-capable IPTV player + enough local storage.**

That is a much more useful way to think about it than assuming the subscription itself contains a cloud DVR.

## Can TiviLive Record Live TV?

TiviLive does not currently list “DVR” or “cloud recording” as a standard subscription feature on its public pricing page.

What it does list is live TV, EPG, VOD, PPV and sports content, 4K/UHD/FHD quality, device compatibility and 24/7 live chat support. The provider’s separate player guide explains that some compatible IPTV players can schedule recordings.

That difference is important when choosing an IPTV with recording.

For example, a player such as TiviMate can use the EPG to schedule a recording, while the recording file is saved to supported local or attached storage. In other words, your IPTV provider delivers the stream; the player does the DVR-style job.

And this is where the boring little details suddenly become very important. Your device needs to remain connected during the recording, and your storage needs to have enough free space. A one-hour HD recording is not exactly a tiny text file.
